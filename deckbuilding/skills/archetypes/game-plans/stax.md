# Game Plan: Stax / Resource Denial

Slow opponents down by taxing, restricting, or denying their resources asymmetrically. While opponents struggle to cast spells, attack, or untap, the deck builds toward its win condition undisturbed.

---

## How the Game Plays Out

**Early:** Deploy stax pieces as early as possible. The earlier a tax lands, the more cumulative damage it deals.

**Mid:** Compound restrictions. Each additional stax piece multiplies the effect. Opponents spend turns removing your pieces instead of advancing their gameplan.

**Late:** Win through an alternate win condition that operates under the lock (combat, combo, or alt win con card).

---

## Breaking Parity

**The defining requirement of Stax:** the stax pieces must hurt opponents more than they hurt you. Every card must be evaluated against this.

Examples of asymmetric stax:
- Smothering Tithe — others pay {2} or you get Treasure; artifact-heavy decks untap artifacts anyway
- Winter Orb — tapping it at end of opponent's turn means you untap normally
- Seedborn Muse — untap on every player's turn; opponents are taxed by their own lands not untapping
- Thorn of Amethyst — spells cost {1} more; artifact-heavy decks cast fewer spells anyway

---

## What the Deck Needs

- **Asymmetric stax pieces** — 6–10 cards that restrict opponents more than you
- **A way to break parity** — enchantments, artifacts, or abilities that let you ignore your own restrictions
- **A win condition that works under the lock** — combat creatures, combo pieces, or alt win con cards that don't require the things you're taxing
- **Removal for problem permanents** — opponents WILL answer your stax; need recursion or redundancy
- **Political awareness** — stax draws enormous aggro; need early diplomacy or protection

---

## Interaction Requirements

- **Balanced to Proactive** — once stax is established, less interaction needed; need early interaction to survive the deployment window
- Prioritize permanent-based interaction (Darksteel Mutation, Song of the Dryads) over removal — stax decks want their interaction to stick, not be discarded

---

## Common Vulnerabilities

- Mass enchantment/artifact removal (Bane of Progress, Vandalblast) resets the lock
- The whole table focuses you — stax draws more hate than any other game plan
- Stax pieces are symmetrical until broken; mistimed deployment hurts you as much as opponents
- Rule of Law shuts down your own spell-based win conditions

- **Win Cons:** Any — stax is how you survive to execute; the win con is separate

# Game Plan: Politics / Group Hug

Influence the game through deals, shared value, and table dynamics rather than direct force. Win by leveraging goodwill, accumulated advantages from political capital, or a late game position opponents helped you reach.

---

## How the Game Plays Out

**Early:** Generate shared value. Cards that help everyone (Howling Mine, Tempt with Discovery) establish you as a non-threatening player.

**Mid:** Make deals. Trade protection from aggression for favors — "I'll let you draw those cards if you leave me alone." Direct damage to aggressive players. Offer targeted assistance to players behind.

**Late:** Pivot to winning. Most political decks have a hidden or announced win condition that the goodwill phase was building toward. Opponents who thought you were harmless find out otherwise.

---

## Two Distinct Expressions

**Group Hug** — pure shared value; everyone draws more, gets more resources, has more fun. Win condition is usually condition-based or combat after extreme card advantage.
- Key cards: Howling Mine, Heartbeat of Spring, Eladamri's Vineyard, Kami of the Crescent Moon

**Political Control** — weaponize social dynamics. Give opponents choices that benefit you, punish aggression diplomatically, build coalitions.
- Key cards: Goad effects (Disrupt Decorum, Grenzo, Havoc Raiser), Tempt cards (Tempt with Reflections), Monarch (Marchesa the Black Rose), voting cards (Council's Dilemma)

---

## What the Deck Needs

- **Value generators that benefit others** — builds goodwill and prevents early targeting
- **Goad effects** — force opponents to attack each other; Disrupt Decorum, Shiny Impetus, Martial Impetus
- **A believable "harmless" phase** — if the table sees the win condition immediately, the political phase fails
- **A decisive win condition** — political decks need to close cleanly once they pivot; vague "I'll win eventually" doesn't work

---

## Interaction Requirements

- **Balanced** — light interaction that doesn't break the political persona; removal that targets aggressive players wins more political capital than random removal
- Prioritize indirect interaction (Tempt cards, goad) over hard removal

---

## Common Vulnerabilities

- Kingmaking — political players often decide who wins rather than winning themselves
- Group hug can accelerate opponents into their win conditions faster than yours
- Table memory — if the deck has won politically before, opponents play around the pivot
- Nebulous win condition leads to games that drag on

## Pairs Well With

- **Engines:** Any — politics is a game plan, not an engine
- **Win Cons:** Alternate Win Cons (condition-based closes naturally from a position of accumulated advantage), Life Depletion (combat after goading opponents into attacking each other)

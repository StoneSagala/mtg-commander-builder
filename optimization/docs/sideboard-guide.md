# Sideboard Guide

Reference for building sideboards in 60-card formats. Commander is singleton with no sideboard — this guide is for Standard, Pioneer, Modern, and similar.

---

## Core Principle

A sideboard covers your 3 worst matchups without diluting your main-deck identity. The 15 slots are not a junk drawer — every card earns its slot against a specific threat category.

---

## How to Build a Sideboard

### Step 1: Identify Your Worst Matchups

Before building the sideboard, know what beats you:
- What strategies beat you before you establish your game plan?
- What permanent types is your maindeck weakest against?
- What interaction does your maindeck lack entirely?

### Step 2: Categorize Threats

| Threat Category | Sideboard Answers |
|----------------|------------------|
| Graveyard-based decks | Rest in Peace, Leyline of the Void, Tormod's Crypt, Relic of Progenitus |
| Aggro / go-wide | Sweepers, lifegain, fog effects |
| Control / counterspells | Hexproof threats, split second spells, planeswalkers |
| Combo decks | Counterspells, discard, hate pieces |
| Artifacts | Shatter effects, Collector Ouphe |
| Enchantments | Naturalize variants, Heliod's Intervention |
| Land-based strategies | Blood Moon, Ghost Quarter, Field of Ruin |

### Step 3: Distribute Slots

**Rule of 3:** Target 3 copies of key hate cards, not 1. A single copy you'll never see isn't a plan.

**Coverage over depth:** 3 cards against matchup A + 3 against B + 3 against C beats 9 cards against one matchup.

**Flexible over narrow:** Cards that answer 2+ matchup categories outperform one-dimensional hate.

---

## By Archetype

### Aggro Sideboards
Post-board, aggro decks add:
- Anti-sweeper technology (indestructible creatures, instant-speed threats)
- Lifegain disruption against control
- Graveyard hate if the meta has resilient recursive threats

Cut: Slow threats that don't improve speed against the specific opponent.

### Control Sideboards
Post-board, control decks add:
- Answers to resolved permanents their counterspells miss
- Additional win conditions against decks that fight their primary win con
- Artifact/enchantment answers

Cut: Reactive cards that are dead against certain opponents.

### Combo Sideboards
Post-board, combo decks add:
- Interaction against hate cards (removal, counterspells)
- Alternative win conditions when the primary combo is disrupted
- Speed cards to dodge disruption windows

Cut: Slow or redundant pieces that aren't needed when the opponent is sideboarding against you.

### Midrange Sideboards
Post-board, midrange decks add:
- Role-players that upgrade the specific matchup
- Catch-all removal that answers unexpected threats

Cut: Cards that are good in the abstract but don't match up against this specific opponent.

---

## Sideboard Decisions During Play

**Game 2 on the draw:** Side in more than on the play — you're reacting, so reactive cards gain value.

**Don't over-sideboard:** Swapping 10+ cards destabilizes your deck's coherence. 5–7 cards in/out is typical.

**Know your cut list:** Have a clear list of what to cut for each matchup before game 2 starts. Don't cut cards that are good — cut cards that are dead or slow against this opponent.
